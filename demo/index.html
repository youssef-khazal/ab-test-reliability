How this is computed

Standard tool: z = difference of log click rates divided by its noise, √(1/clicks − 1/views) for each version; significant if |z| > 1.96 (p < 0.05).

Is that noise formula right? It was checked on 161 Upworthy tests that contained identical versions: a 5% test called 5.7% of them significant, and their differences were 1.14 times as spread as the formula says (95% CI 0.96-1.34). The standard formula is therefore used as is.

B truly better, real gain: empirical Bayes. The distribution of true differences between two versions was estimated from 175,186 version pairs (nonparametric maximum likelihood). Your measurement is combined with it to give the probability that B is better and the likely size of its gain. Small tests are pulled strongly towards "no difference"; large tests hardly at all.

Views needed: (1.96 + 0.84)² × (1/p_A + 1/p_B) / log(1 + gain)², at A's click rate.
